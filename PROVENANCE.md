## Sample data

All sample data is synthetic. It's made by the browser at run time, so no data file ships with the app.

- Generator: `src/lib/sample.ts`, using the mulberry32 seeded random number generator.
- Seeds and company names (all made up):

| Industry | Seed | Sample company |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurant | 12 | Sample Restaurant Group |
| Construction | 7 | Sample Builders Group |
| Fleet | 20 | Sample Freight Lines |
| Real estate | 22 | Sample Property Group |
| General (any other industry) | 31 | Sample Company |

- Valuation date for samples: Oct 31, 2026.
- Location labels use generic city names and invented store, project, terminal and property numbers. They don't refer to real sites.
- There are no personal names. Claimants show as labels like "Guest" or "Third party" or a made-up employee ID (EE-12345), and drivers and units are made-up IDs (D-1471).
- The same seed always produces the same claims, so anyone can rebuild the sample and compare.

### How the sample follows BLS patterns

The sample is still made up, but its shape follows national BLS statistics so it looks like a real loss run:

| What | BLS source | How it's used |
|---|---|---|
| Comp cause mix | SOII 2023-2024, Table R4 (DART cases by event or exposure) | Weights for falls, overexertion, struck-by, transportation and so on, per industry |
| Body part | SOII 2023-2024, Table R2 (DART cases by part of body) | Weights for picking a body part within each cause |
| Nature of injury | SOII 2023-2024, Table R1 (DART cases by nature) | Weights for sprain, cut, burn, fracture and so on within each cause |
| Case type odds | SOII 2024, Table 1 (DAFW, DJTR and other recordable rates) | Days away vs restricted vs other recordable, tilted by how long that injury usually keeps people out |
| Days away and restricted | SOII 2023-2024, Tables R65 and R67 (median days) | Median days by industry and by nature, with spread around the median |
| Hours worked | SOII 2024, Table 1 (TRC rate) | Hours are set so the sample company runs about 10% above its industry rate |

Template assumptions (not from BLS, chosen for the sample): 82% of comp claims with a payment are recordable; lost-time claims cost about 1.75 times the category median, restricted 1.15 times, other recordable 0.62 times, and non-recordable 0.3 times; offices log 2.5 times the hours per injury of field sites; heat vs electrical exposure in construction is split evenly. These only shape the demo data. They don't touch the math on a user's own loss run.

## Benchmark data

- `src/lib/naics.ts` and `data/bls-soii-2024-table1-rates.csv` hold BLS SOII Table 1, "Incidence rates of nonfatal occupational injuries and illnesses by industry and case types, 2024": every row BLS published with a total recordable rate (1,009 private industry codes, plus the totals and the state and local government rows). Blank cells mean BLS didn't publish that number. Footnote marks were removed. Copied Nov 13, 2026 from https://www.bls.gov/iif/nonfatal-injuries-and-illnesses-tables/table-1-injury-and-illness-rates-by-industry-2024-national.htm
- The copy was checked against the BLS page with a checksum of the whole table.
- BLS data is a U.S. government work and in the public domain.
- `src/lib/naics-titles.ts` holds the 2,196 titles of the 2017 North American Industry Classification System from the U.S. Census Bureau, plus up to 22 keywords per 6-digit industry taken from the Census descriptions (filler words and cross-references removed). The Census file was read from the `naics` npm package, version 1.2.1 (MIT license, Nathan Dalbec), which ships the Census "2017_NAICS_Descriptions" data. Only the data was used, not the package code. Census data is in the public domain.
- The General template's comp cause mix is the average of the four BLS industry mixes (restaurants, construction, trucking, real estate), by share. Its body part and nature weights add those four industries together.

Test files used during development (an Excel carrier-style loss run, a CSV export and a Word table) were also generated by script with made-up values. They are not part of this repo.

## Default risk levels and their sources

Every level can be changed in the app under Risk levels. Each row in that window shows where its default came from, and the PowerPoint export lists the levels used.

| Setting | Default | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Average days from loss to report | Low 3, Guarded 7, Moderate 14, High 30 (Severe above) | Gallagher / PRIMA (3-day standard), The Hartford, NCCI 2015 |
| Location points for average report lag | Over 14 days | NCCI 2015 (costs climb from week 3) |
| Late reporting finding | Share of claims reported after 7 days | The Hartford (costs rise in week 2) |
| Everything else (change bands, litigation, location scoring, spikes, large loss $100,000, aging) | See `src/lib/thresholds.ts` | Template starting point, not a published benchmark |

Sources:

1. NCCI research brief, "The Relationship Between Accident Report Lag and Claim Cost in Workers Compensation Insurance" (2015), as reported by Business Insurance. Week 3 claims cost about 35% more than week 2, and up to about 51% more by week 4. Attorney involvement went from 12.8% (same day) to 31.7% (4+ weeks). https://www.businessinsurance.com/reporting-delays-tied-to-higher-workers-comp-claim-costs/
2. The Hartford claim report lag study, as cited by Arthur J. Gallagher on the PRIMA blog. Week 2 claims cost about 18% more than week 1; after week 4, about 45% more. https://primacentral.org/education/podcasts-blog/a-new-approach-to-controlling-lag-time/
3. Arthur J. Gallagher on the PRIMA blog: many organizations use a three-day reporting standard. Experts quoted by Business Insurance call 24 to 48 hours optimal. Same URLs as above.

"Template starting point" means the number is a reasonable first guess picked for this template and tested against the sample data. It isn't an industry standard. Users should set their own to match their program, carrier guidance or company policy.

## How to check it yourself

1. No network: open the offline file, press F12, open the Network tab, then use the app. You'll see the file itself plus `data:` and `blob:` entries, and nothing from the web. The first tag in the file is a Content-Security-Policy with `connect-src data: blob:`, so the browser refuses any web request even if code tried.
2. Works with no internet: turn Wi-Fi off and load a loss run. Everything still works.
3. No AI in offline files: `bash build-offline.sh` swaps `src/lib/claude.ts` for `src/lib/claude.offline.ts` before bundling. Then search the built files:
   ```
   grep -c "You help set up a branded" offline/*.html
   ```
   It should print 0. The same text appears once in the claude.ai build (`bundle.sh` without `LOSS_OFFLINE=1`), where the optional AI color lookup lives.
4. No hidden data: search the built files for any company or claimant name you care about. The only company names are the five sample names above.
5. Same sample every time: the seeds above live in `src/lib/industries.ts`. Rebuild and the sample claims match.
